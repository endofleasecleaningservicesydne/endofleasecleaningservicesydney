Kitchen Cleaning Checklist Before Moving Out

Moving out can be stressful, and the kitchen is usually one of the most time-consuming areas to clean. A structured checklist helps you cover the obvious mess as well as the easy-to-miss grease, crumbs and grime that can affect the final inspection.

End of lease cleaning is more than a routine tidy-up. It is the final detailed clean completed before a tenant hands the property back, and the kitchen is usually one of the most important areas because grease, food residue, spills and hidden grime can build up over time. A structured approach helps make the property look well presented for the final inspection and reduces the chance of obvious cleaning issues being left behind.

This guide focuses specifically on end of lease kitchen cleaning, with a practical checklist for ovens, stovetops, rangehoods, cupboards, appliances, sinks, floors and the easy-to-miss areas that often need extra attention before vacating a rental property.

End of Lease Kitchen Cleaning Checklist

  • Remove all food, rubbish and personal belongings
  • Clean the oven, racks, trays and glass door
  • Degrease the stovetop, burners, trivets and knobs
  • Clean the rangehood and filters
  • Wipe inside and outside all cupboards and drawers
  • Clean pantry shelves and storage areas
  • Wash benchtops and splashbacks
  • Scrub the sink, drain and taps
  • Clean the fridge, freezer, microwave and dishwasher if supplied
  • Wipe doors, handles, switches and visible wall marks
  • Clean windows, frames, tracks and sills
  • Dust skirting boards, vents and corners
  • Clean behind movable appliances where safe
  • Vacuum, sweep and mop the floor
  • Complete a final inspection before returning the keys

1. Empty the Kitchen Before Cleaning

Remove food, containers, rubbish and personal belongings before you start. Empty cupboards, drawers, pantry shelves and appliances so that every surface can be cleaned properly. This matters during an end of lease clean because inspectors may look inside cupboards, appliances and storage areas that are hidden during everyday use.

2. Work From Top to Bottom

Start with high surfaces such as the tops of cupboards, shelves and the rangehood. Then work down to benchtops, appliances, skirting boards and floors. This helps prevent dust and residue from falling onto areas you have already cleaned.

3. Deep Clean the Oven

The oven is often one of the most time-consuming areas of an end of lease clean. Remove racks and trays and clean baked-on grease, food residue and carbon buildup from the interior. Wipe the door, glass, handle, seals, knobs and exterior as well.

4. Degrease the Stovetop

Remove loose crumbs and clean around burners, trivets, knobs and cooktop edges. If your cooktop has removable parts, wash them separately. For glass or ceramic surfaces, avoid abrasive tools that could scratch the finish.

5. Clean the Rangehood and Filters

Rangehoods collect airborne grease and dust. Wipe the exterior, underside and controls, and clean washable filters according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Also check the nearby splashback and wall surface for greasy residue.

6. Clean Cupboards, Drawers and Pantry Shelves

Vacuum crumbs first, then wipe the inside and outside of every cupboard and drawer. Pay attention to handles, hinges, shelf edges and corners. Upper cabinet tops should also be checked because grease and dust often collect there.

7. Clean Benchtops and Splashbacks

Remove all small appliances and objects so the entire benchtop is accessible. Clean grease, stains and sticky residue, then wipe the splashback, especially behind the stove and sink. Use products suitable for the surface material.

8. Scrub the Sink, Drain and Taps

Clean the sink basin, drain, plug and tap base. Remove water marks and residue, then dry the area with a clean cloth. A polished sink can make the whole kitchen look noticeably cleaner during the final inspection.

9. Clean Included Appliances

If the refrigerator, freezer, dishwasher, microwave or other appliances belong to the rental property, clean them inside and out. Remove food residue, spills, odours and fingerprints, and return shelves and removable parts to their correct positions.

10. Clean Behind and Under Appliances

Dust, crumbs and grease can collect behind fridges, freestanding ovens and other movable appliances. Clean these areas where it is safe and practical to do so. Do not move heavy or connected appliances if there is a risk of injury or property damage.

11. Wipe Walls, Doors and High-Touch Areas

Spot-clean visible marks on walls and wipe doors, handles, switches, cupboard pulls and other frequently touched areas. Keep excess moisture away from electrical fittings and use products suitable for painted surfaces.

12. Clean Windows, Tracks and Sills

Clean the glass, frames and sills, and remove dirt from window tracks. If the kitchen has blinds or shutters, dust and wipe them where needed.

13. Dust Skirting Boards, Vents and Corners

Vacuum loose dust from skirting boards, vents and corners before wiping visible marks. Check ceiling corners and other less obvious areas for cobwebs.

14. Vacuum and Mop the Floor Last

Vacuum or sweep around cabinets, appliance edges and corners, then mop with a product appropriate for the flooring material. Let the floor dry before walking back through the kitchen.

15. Remove Rubbish and Perform a Final Check

Empty bins and recycling before leaving. Once the kitchen is dry, inspect it from the doorway, open cupboards and drawers, check the oven and rangehood, and look at shiny surfaces under good light for streaks or fingerprints.

Areas Commonly Missed During End of Lease Cleaning

  • Top of the refrigerator
  • Top of wall cupboards
  • Rangehood filters and underside
  • Oven door edges and glass
  • Drawer runners and cupboard handles
  • Behind the kitchen bin
  • Under movable appliances
  • Window tracks
  • Skirting boards near cooking areas
  • Light switches and power-point surrounds
  • Sink drain and tap base

Why End of Lease Cleaning Is More Detailed Than Regular Cleaning

Routine house cleaning is designed to keep a property comfortable for everyday living. End of lease cleaning is more thorough because the property is being prepared for handover. That usually means cleaning inside cupboards, appliance interiors, window tracks, hidden edges and other areas that may not be part of a normal weekly clean.

How Long Does End of Lease Kitchen Cleaning Take?

The time required depends on the size and condition of the kitchen. A well-maintained kitchen may take a few hours, while a kitchen with heavy grease buildup, a dirty oven and several appliances may take much longer. Starting early gives cleaning products time to work and allows shelves, racks and filters to dry properly.

DIY End of Lease Cleaning or Professional Cleaning?

DIY cleaning can be practical if you have enough time, suitable products and a manageable amount of buildup. Professional cleaning can be useful when the property is large, the kitchen is heavily soiled or your moving schedule is tight.

End of Lease Cleaning Tips Before the Final Inspection

  • Start before moving day: Avoid leaving the entire clean until the final few hours.
  • Review your condition report: Compare the current condition with the entry condition report where relevant.
  • Check your tenancy requirements: Review any cleaning obligations in your lease and applicable local rules.
  • Use suitable products: Avoid damaging stone, timber, stainless steel or painted finishes.
  • Clean hidden areas: Inspect cupboards, appliance edges, window tracks and skirting boards.
  • Take photos after cleaning: A dated visual record can help document the condition at handover.
  • Complete a final walkthrough: Check the entire property before returning the keys.

Frequently Asked Questions About End of Lease Cleaning

What is included in end of lease cleaning?

End of lease cleaning generally involves detailed cleaning of kitchens, bathrooms, floors, fixtures, cupboards, surfaces and other areas of the rental property. Exact requirements vary depending on the property, tenancy agreement and applicable local rules.

Do tenants need to clean the oven before moving out?

If the oven is part of the rental property, it should generally be left clean and free from obvious grease and food residue, taking into account the property’s initial condition and relevant tenancy requirements.

Should cupboards and drawers be cleaned inside?

Yes. Empty them completely, remove crumbs and wipe the internal surfaces, doors, handles and shelf edges.

Do I need to clean behind appliances?

Clean behind and underneath movable appliances where it is safe and practical. Avoid moving connected or heavy appliances if doing so could cause injury or damage.

Is end of lease cleaning the same as regular house cleaning?

No. End of lease cleaning is usually more detailed and includes areas that may not be part of routine weekly cleaning, such as oven interiors, inside cupboards, window tracks and hidden edges.

Final Thoughts

End of lease cleaning should be approached as a detailed property handover clean rather than a routine tidy-up. In the kitchen, focus on grease, appliance interiors, cupboards, hidden areas and floors, then complete a final check before returning the keys.

A systematic clean can help you present the rental property in a tidy, well-maintained condition and reduce the chance of obvious cleaning issues being raised during the final inspection.
